Temperature in LLMs

What it is: Temperature is a parameter that controls the randomness of the output generated by a Large Language Model (LLM) like GPT. It influences the probability distribution of the next token the model predicts.
How it works: LLMs predict the next word in a sequence by assigning probabilities to all possible words. Without temperature, the model always picks the most likely word. Temperature adjusts these probabilities.
Low Temperature (e.g., 0.2): The model becomes more deterministic. It strongly favors the highest probability word, leading to predictable, focused, and conservative outputs. It’s good for tasks needing accuracy and consistency.
High Temperature (e.g., 1.0): The model becomes more random. It gives more weight to less likely words, resulting in more creative, surprising, and potentially nonsensical outputs. Useful for brainstorming, creative writing, or exploring different possibilities.
Temperature of 0: This is the most deterministic setting. The model will always choose the single most probable token at each step. It effectively removes randomness.
Under the Hood: The LLM’s internal softmax function calculates probabilities for each possible token. Temperature scales these probabilities before the softmax is applied. The higher the temperature, the less the softmax concentrates probability mass on the highest scores.
